Which station-space experiences go with passenger ratings?

Singapore North-South Line (27 stations) · Google Maps reviews captured 4 Oct 2026 · BPS5231 · Wang Yulu and Zhang Xinyue

1. Data

Reviews were collected newest-first for all 27 stations. Most records are rating-only, and the model uses the 3,884 text reviews that mention a station-space aspect.

Counts match the displayed total at 26 stations; Yew Tee returned 145 of 146. Online reviewers are self-selected, so this is not a random sample of passengers.

In this model vertical access (V) is rarely mentioned but ranks second, while cleanliness (Q) is the reverse. The table below shows how far these ranks hold across models.

Importance here is predictive reliance in a fitted model, not a benefit of changing the space. A negative or near-zero value only means that shuffling did not hurt this model's predictions; it does not mean the issue does not matter in practice. Rankings also differ between the logistic and random forest models.

How stable are the ranks?

Share of 300 station resamples in which each aspect ranks in the top three, for mention-only (B) and aspect-plus-polarity (C) models, next to how often a negative mention comes with a 1–3★ rating (all reviews: 20%).

Connectivity (C) and amenities (A) occupy the top three point-estimate ranks in all four spatial models shown here (B and C). Within these four models, vertical access (V) ranks in the top three consistently only in mention-only logistic regression; cleanliness (Q) moves to first once polarity is added, because “clean” and “dirty” carry sentiment. Polarity is coded by fixed rules (see section 5). Resamples reuse fixed out-of-fold predictions.

3. Do the spatial features carry rating information?

All models were evaluated on whole held-out stations (grouped 5-fold, three seeded runs that form two distinct partitions). High ratings are 80% of the sample, so accuracy alone is misleading and macro F1 and AUC are shown.

Adding polarity (C) raises AUC by about 0.11–0.13 over mention flags (station-bootstrap intervals above zero), but a control with mention flags plus whole-review sentiment does slightly better, so these rules do not show that knowing which aspect was praised or criticised adds information beyond the overall tone.

AUC is a ranking score, not percent accuracy. Spatial mention flags carry modest information; the text model is stronger but also uses general sentiment words, so it is a comparison point and not a diagnosis of physical causes. Mention flags record that an aspect was mentioned, not whether it was praised or criticised.

5. How far the labels have been checked

  • Aspect coding in the model uses a fixed English/Chinese keyword dictionary. It cannot tell praise from complaint.
  • One AI read a stratified 200-review sample with ratings hidden: 90 station-space, 22 unclear, 88 other. These counts are not population shares. Agreement with the dictionary on station-space scope is 61 + 68 of 160 reviews (about 81%), with detection of individual aspects weaker (e.g. connectivity: 15 of 32 AI-positive records found by the dictionary).
  • Independent human coding: both authors coded the same reviews with ratings hidden, before any discussion. Round 1 (60 reviews, codebook v1): scope κ = 0.26, aspect mention κ = 0.47 (9 aspects × 60). After revising and freezing the codebook (v2), round 2 on 40 new reviews: scope κ = 0.77, aspect mention κ = 0.67. All 100 reviews were then adjudicated jointly.
  • The dictionary labels used by the model were checked against the 100 adjudicated human labels. Station-space or not: precision 0.77, recall 0.82 (κ = 0.45). Which aspects are mentioned (9 × 100 decisions): precision 0.49, recall 0.61 (κ = 0.49); the largest over-detection is for amenities (A) and walking paths (M).
  • Aspect polarity is coded by fixed rules, checked on the AI development set and then tested once against the 100 adjudicated human labels: 54 aspect instances, raw agreement 0.63, κ = 0.34; the rules find 84% of complaints but only 25% of praise.
  • Earlier 224-review pilot: removing its 13 non-spatial reviews gives F1 0.634 → 0.623 and AUC 0.736 → 0.712 (five repeats each); connectivity, amenities and vertical access remain the top three.
  • Prospective check: after freezing the codebook, rules and models (trained on the other 26 stations), we collected the 36 Woodlands reviews posted after the 4 Oct capture. Only 4 had text that mentions a station-space aspect, all rated 4–5★; each frozen model classified 3 of 4 correctly. The pipeline runs on new reviews, but the sample is too small to estimate performance. Woodlands’ earlier reviews were seen during development.
