1. (25s)
Our starting point is a daily question. We use MRT stations often, but how well do their exits, transfer routes, lifts and toilets support the journey? We examine what passengers have already written, look for recurring experiences and relate them to overall ratings. We then use the review evidence to develop specific proposals.

2. (30s)
We cover all twenty-seven North-South Line stations. We collected over twenty-one thousand rating records, including seven thousand six hundred text reviews. After screening, three thousand eight hundred and eighty-four reviews enter modelling. Most are highly rated. Our questions concern the details: which experiences recur, which accompany lower ratings, and what practical proposals the comments can support.

3. (45s)
AI makes a large set of comments easier to compare. A dictionary and clause rules encode nine experiences and positive or negative wording. Logistic regression and random forests learn their relationship with ratings. Both authors independently checked one hundred comments, and test stations stay outside model fitting. Adding polarity improves macro F1 from point five five to point six five. Overall tone performs slightly better, so we return to the original comments before proposing changes.

4. (35s)
Amenities and connections to surrounding destinations are mentioned most. They also rank consistently near the top across our spatial models. Less frequent topics can still deserve attention. Vertical access appears in only about four percent of reviews. Among its rule-coded negative mentions, sixty-eight percent are rated one to three stars. For cleanliness the share is sixty-one percent. We next examine what these comments refer to at individual stations.

5. (32s)
At Dhoby Ghaut, eighty-seven model reviews mention wayfinding or vertical access. Fourteen percent of this subset are one or two stars, compared with eight percent for the station. Reviewers describe difficult exits and complicated transfers, while others praise clear signs. Our candidate proposal is therefore targeted: add exit numbers and destination cues at the main transfer decision points. Some reviews praise clear signs, supporting a focus on specific decision points.

6. (32s)
At Jurong East, two hundred and twenty-six model reviews mention wayfinding, crowding or circulation. Their one-to-two-star share is fourteen percent, versus eleven percent for the station. These comments contain several different problems. We separate crowding from platform confusion and construction detours. The candidate proposal starts with information: show the platform before passengers choose a route and clarify directions towards destinations. Positive reviews describe spacious, tidy areas. Information cues offer a focused starting point.

7. (32s)
At Kranji, fifty-six model reviews mention cleanliness and upkeep. Sixteen percent are one or two stars, compared with ten percent station-wide. The quotations identify dirty toilets, missing supplies and odour near bins. We propose checking toilet cleaning and restocking, and clarifying who manages the bin area. These are review-based candidate proposals. This project did not include site visits. Retain clean and well-maintained accounts to distinguish places and situations.

8. (36s)
The website makes the findings usable. Select a station and inspect its reviews, then click Run this station. In the game, allocate a limited budget to signs, cleaning or other measures. Read the daily feedback and adjust the next choice. The reviews are real evidence. Game outcomes follow illustrative rules, so the game communicates trade-offs rather than predicting the real effect of an intervention.

9. (33s)
The project turns a broad rating into a more specific discussion. We organise nine spatial experiences, use AI to compare their associations with ratings, and check coding errors against human labels. We then connect recurring patterns to quotations, counterexamples and candidate actions. Our outputs are a reproducible analysis workflow, three station cases and an interactive website. Further work can improve context recognition and test it on fresh reviews.
